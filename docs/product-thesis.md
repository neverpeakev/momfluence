## The thesis, in one paragraph

**The rules of influencer marketing just changed.** It used to be that brands
only paid creators with 100K+ followers. That world is gone. Today, big
brands quietly pay regular moms — moms with 500 followers, no application,
no portfolio — **20–50% of every sale they bring in.** The infrastructure
exists. The money exists. The mom is already eligible. **Nobody told her.**

Momfluence's job is to be the one who tells her, and then make the path from
*"wait, really?"* to *"I just made $40"* frictionless. One login. One link.
Curated programs. $5/month to access.

---

## THE canonical line (use verbatim or as close as possible)

> **Big brands quietly started paying moms with 500 followers up to 50%
> of every sale they bring in. We're the platform that makes it easy.**

This is the gold-standard hook. Two sentences:

1. **The news** — quiet, factual, specific. "Quietly started" gives the
   reader the feeling they're being let in on something. "500 followers"
   makes her think *"wait — I have more than 500 followers."* "50%"
   anchors the size of the prize against the 1% she might know from
   Amazon Associates. "Every sale they bring in" makes it concrete.
2. **The positioning** — short, confident, low-friction. We're the rails,
   not the gatekeeper.

Every ad headline, every LP hero, every page meta description, every
press blurb should start from this line and deviate only when there's a
specific reason. The autonomous post generator may compose around it
(e.g., add a third sentence with a personal-anecdote hook) but should
not water it down with abstraction.

## Who we're talking to

A mom who:

- Is **already** a trusted recommender. Her group chats, school WhatsApp,
  Nextdoor, mom Facebook groups, neighborhood Facebook marketplace — she's
  the one people ask "what brand of [X] do you use?"
- Has 500–5,000 friends/followers on her existing socials. Built up over
  years from middle school, high school, college, mom-life friendships.
  Not a "creator." Not an "influencer." Just a real person with a real life.
- **Knows** that other women — influencers, celebrities, "official" mom
  influencers — make money pushing products online. She gets that this is
  possible in the world. She just assumes it's not possible *for her* because
  she doesn't have a personal brand and hasn't been "doing the work."
- Is curious, active online, comfortable posting opinions, may follow a bunch
  of mom influencers (official and unofficial), may even fantasize about
  starting her own thing one day — but **has not done it yet**.
- Is feeling household-cost pressure. Is open to making side money. Doesn't
  want a second job. Doesn't think of herself as "needing to make money"
  desperately but absolutely *wants* it.
- Has **zero knowledge** of affiliate marketing as a category. Doesn't know
  the word "affiliate link." Doesn't know that 35 separate networks exist
  with applications and minimums and rules. She doesn't know what she
  doesn't know.

**She is not the influencer. She is the next 100 million people who didn't
know they were eligible to be one.**

## The "huh, really?" test

Every piece of content has ONE job: produce this internal monologue in the
reader's head:

> *"Wait — brands will pay me to recommend their stuff? With 600 followers?
> Without applying anywhere? **Why didn't anyone tell me this before?**"*

If a piece of content doesn't produce that beat, it's filler. Cut it.

This is **revelation marketing, not pain-point marketing.** Do not lead with
"are you tired of." Do not lead with "wish you could make extra money."
Lead with the new fact she didn't know.

Comparable category-creating messaging:

- *"Your savings account is paying 0.01%. Online savings accounts pay 4%."*
  — Marcus, Ally. Not pain, just a fact you can't un-hear.
- *"You're spending $35/mo on subscriptions you forgot about."*
  — Rocket Money. Not pain, just a number.
- *"Brands pay 20–50% commissions to whoever brings them a customer.
  Amazon pays you 1%."* — Momfluence, this thesis.

## The three "news beats" we test

Every ad, post, and LP hero tests ONE of these three sub-angles:

### 1. The Amount
*"20–50% of every sale. Not 1% like Amazon affiliates."*
Anchors the size of the prize. Wakes up anyone who has heard "affiliate"
before and assumed it was nickels.

### 2. The Eligibility
*"500 followers. No application. No portfolio. You're already in."*
Defeats the #1 internal objection: "but I'm not an influencer."

### 3. The Simplicity
*"One link. Share it where you already share things. We handle the rest."*
Defeats the #2 internal objection: "this sounds like a lot of work."

Every Claude-generated post must be tagged with which beat it tests
(`amount` / `eligibility` / `simplicity`). The weekly audit rolls up by
beat to tell us which is winning.

## The qualifier (front-and-center)

We **lead with the $5/mo membership fee in the ad itself.** Not buried.
Not later. The qualifier sounds like:

> *"$5/mo to access. 95% of momfluencers turn that $5 into at least $25
> profit on day one. If you aren't willing to spend $5 to make $25 — or
> potentially $2,500+ in month one — please don't click."*

This filters out:
- Tire-kickers who never convert
- "Make money online" tourists with no intent
- People who confuse us with free side-hustle gimmicks

This *attracts*:
- Women who self-identify as serious about trying
- Women who respect that we respect their time
- Women who like that we have the guts to say it out loud

Net effect: **our CPA goes down**, not up, because we're paying for
qualified clicks only. This is intentional.

## What we are NOT

- ❌ **Not an MLM.** But: we do not lead with "we're not an MLM." That's
  defensive. Defensive marketing addresses objections people don't have
  yet. We only address this in FAQ and post-click LP content if asked.
- ❌ **Not a "side hustle" app.** That phrase is downmarket, signals
  cheap-gig-economy. We don't use it. We use "earn from what you already
  share" or just "make money."
- ❌ **Not a "personal brand" platform.** Building a brand is a downstream
  *option* — some moms will arrive there, some won't. We don't gatekeep
  either way. Don't make "personal brand" a prerequisite in any copy.
- ❌ **Not a "time fit" gig** (DoorDash, Instacart, etc.). She's not looking
  for "fits between school dropoff and pickup." Stop framing it that way.
- ❌ **Not patronizing.** Don't tell her "moms are powerful" — she already
  knows. Telling her is talking down. Just deliver the fact and trust her
  to do the math.

## Tone

- Shaan-Puri-style. Plain-spoken. Direct. Specific numbers over abstractions.
- Confident without being braggy. ("$25 on day one for 95% of momfluencers"
  is a confident *number*, not a hype claim.)
- Slightly self-aware. ("Yes, there's a fee. Here's why. Here's the math.")
- Conversational asides are fine. ("…the kind of thing a Sephora regional
  manager would pay to know.")
- Short paragraphs. White space. Mobile-readable.
- One emoji per post, max. Used purposefully. Never strings.
- Never ALL CAPS for emphasis.
- Never "Real talk" or "Hot take" as openers — overused.

---

## What the user has NOT signed up for

Things we do NOT promise, period:

- Specific $/day or $/week earnings (FTC compliance)
- Quitting their job
- Replacing income
- "Easy money," "passive income" without qualification, "no effort"
- "Get rich"

Things we CAN say:

- Specific provable facts (e.g., "First $25 unlocks day one" — true,
  it's how the fast-track payout works)
- "95% of momfluencers earn at least $25 in their first day" — only if
  this is actually verifiable from our data
- "Up to 50% of each sale" — true, varies by program
- "Recurring monthly commissions on subscriptions" — true for most of
  our programs

## The funnel split

There are two distinct conversation phases. Different copy jobs:

### Phase 1 — Ads + organic social (revelation)
Job: produce the "huh, really?" beat. Earn the click. The reader hasn't
asked for this yet. Lead with the new fact, qualified by the $5/mo.

### Phase 2 — Landing page + signup (problem-solving)
Job: she's curious now. She'll have objections. Address them. *Now*
problem-solution copy works because she's articulated the problems to
herself. "Wait, is this legit?" "Is it $5 forever?" "What if I don't
have any followers?" → LPs answer these explicitly.

Same product. Two voices.
