## Pattern analysis

### 1. Format monoculture (severe — the whole point of the taxonomy)

**7/7 posts are `anecdote`.** Zero `direct`, zero `math`, zero `brand-callout`, zero `objection-reframe`. This is the exact failure mode the format taxonomy was created to prevent. The thesis is explicit that variety comes from format rotation:

> "Variety comes from **how** we tell the same news (anecdote vs. direct vs. math vs. brand-callout vs. objection-reframe), not from what we say."

Root cause: the generator surfaces `recentAngleTags` and `recentDisplays` in the user prompt but never tells Claude which formats have been used recently. Given free choice with no rotation signal, `anecdote` wins every day — it's the format that most obviously licenses the specific-number-plus-specific-place moves the SYSTEM_PROMPT rewards. There is no back-pressure toward the others.

### 2. Headline-shape fossilization

Every headline this week is a variation on **"She named the [PRODUCT] / [PREPOSITION] the [PLACE]."**

- She named the motion sickness bands / at the field trip bus loading
- She named the hairspray / at the cheer competition
- She named the baby wipes / in the pumpkin patch hayride line
- She named the bobby pins / backstage at the dance recital
- She named the shin guard tape / on the soccer sideline
- She named the electrolyte chews / by the starting blocks
- She named the produce bags / in the farmers market parking lot

Any real reader who sees this on the Momfluence FB Page two days in a row clocks it as templated. The generator was shown the recent displays as a "visual rhythm" reference — but with no explicit instruction to break structural rhymes, it treated the rhythm as a target, not something to vary.

### 3. Caption-skeleton fossilization

Every caption opens with **"A regular mom in [CITY] was [gerund] when another mom asked / mentioned..."** and closes with the same eligibility puncture stanza. It's a template with the noun swapped in. The specific-mom-in-specific-place anchor is doing exactly what the anecdote format is supposed to do — but the skeleton around it is not rotating.

### 4. Geographic repeat

Tulsa appears in gen-2026-09-05 (Tuesday) and gen-2026-09-06 (Wednesday) — back-to-back days. The generator has no visibility into recent cities.

### 5. Visual monoculture

`image_bg` distribution: **6/7 warm-gradient, 1/7 coral, 0/7 anything else.** The `navy`, `cream`, `navy-coral-gradient`, `white-coral-ring` options are unused. As with content_format, the generator can't see recent choices and defaults to the same one.

### 6. Caption length distribution

Word counts: 195, 155, 175, 200, 165, 195, 170. Cluster tightly around 170–200 words. Comfortable range for FB, no anomalies.

### 7. `accent_badge` variety

## What this means

Every individual post is on-thesis and scores 5.0. Every post also participates in a category-level failure the per-post axes can't detect. This is exactly what the audit exists to catch, and exactly why the roll-up-by-format matters: the format tag on every row said "anecdote," "anecdote," "anecdote," "anecdote," "anecdote," "anecdote," "anecdote," and no one noticed until the audit ran.

## Recommended intervention

Ship a **prompt-tuning refinement** (not a voice pivot — v5 voice stays locked). See the PR opened alongside this audit:

1. Plumb recent `content_formats` and `image_bgs` through `GeneratorInputs` into the user prompt.
2. Make **format rotation** a hard constraint in SYSTEM_PROMPT: cannot match any of the last 3 formats.
3. Add a retry check that rejects a generation whose `content_format` matches any of the last 3.
4. Explicit instruction against reusing headline structural rhymes from recent displays.
5. Rotate `image_bg` — prefer one not in the recent list.
6. Bump `PROMPT_VERSION` → `2026-09-06.v6`.
7. Update `docs/product-thesis.md` to reflect the rotation constraint in the "Content formats" section.

No BLOCKLIST additions this week. No voice changes. The v5 voice is producing on-model prose; what needs fixing is the axes-of-variation the generator was blind to.
